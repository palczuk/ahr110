Heat, Temperature, and Pressure

Study guide organized by topic, with formulas, field notes and a 25-question knowledge check.

TEMPERATUREHEATPRESSUREHVACR
1.1

Temperature

  • Measures the level or intensity of heat, not total heat content.
  • Water freezes at 32°F (0°C) and boils at 212°F (100°C) at sea level.
  • Absolute zero: -460°F (-273°C), theoretical point where molecular motion ceases.
  • Scales: Fahrenheit, Celsius, Rankine and Kelvin.
1.2

Temperature Conversion Formulas

°F = (1.8 × °C) + 32
°C = (°F − 32) ÷ 1.8
°R = °F + 460   |   K = °C + 273
1.3

Introduction to Heat

  • Heat is energy: it cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed.
  • Heat naturally flows from a warmer substance to a cooler substance.
  • 1 Btu raises 1 pound of water by 1°F.
1.4

Heat Transfer Mechanisms

  • Conduction: molecule-to-molecule transfer through direct contact.
  • Convection: transfer by a moving fluid, natural or forced.
  • Radiation: transfer by electromagnetic waves through space.
1.5

Sensible Heat

  • Heat added or removed that changes temperature without changing state.
  • Easily measured with a standard thermometer.
1.6

Latent Heat

  • Heat that changes state without changing temperature.
  • Vaporization: 970 Btu/lb for water at 212°F.
  • Fusion: 144 Btu/lb for ice/water at 32°F.
1.7

Specific Heat

  • Btus required to raise 1 lb of a substance by 1°F.
  • Water = 1.0 Btu/lb/°F
Q = W × c × ΔT
1.8

Total Heat (Enthalpy)

Total heat content combines sensible and latent heat and is used in HVACR to calculate cooling and dehumidification capacity.

1.9

Atmospheric Pressure

  • Sea-level standard: 14.696 psia or 29.92 in. Hg.
  • Pressure decreases with altitude, lowering the boiling point of liquids.
1.10

Gauge vs. Absolute Pressure

  • psig: reads zero at normal atmospheric pressure.
  • psia: includes atmospheric pressure.
psia = psig + 14.7
psig = psia − 14.7
1.11

Vacuum and Inches of Mercury

Pressures below atmospheric are measured in in. Hg or microns for deep vacuums.

psia = (30 − vacuum in. Hg) ÷ 2
1.12

Pressure–Temperature Relationship

At constant pressure, a liquid boils at a specific temperature. Increasing pressure raises boiling point; lowering pressure lowers it.

1.13

HVACR Pressure Measuring Instruments

  • Bourdon tube gauge: curved brass tube that straightens under pressure.
  • Compound gauge: measures psig above atmosphere and vacuum in. Hg below atmosphere.
  • Digital / micron gauges: high-precision tools for deep vacuum measurement.
